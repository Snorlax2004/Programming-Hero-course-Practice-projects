The Art of Getting Lost: Why We Travel


There is a specific kind of magic that happens the moment you step off a plane or drive past the "Welcome To" sign of a new city. It’s the realization that the world is much larger than our daily routines suggest.

On my recent journey, I reminded myself that travel isn't just about checking landmarks off a bucket list or finding the perfect photo op. It’s about the sensory details: the smell of street food sizzling on a humid evening, the rhythmic sound of a language you don’t quite understand, and the humbling feeling of being a stranger in a beautiful place.

The best moments are rarely the ones we plan. They are the wrong turns that lead to hidden cafes, the conversations with locals that change our perspective and the quiet stillness of a sunrise in a foreign sky. Travel challenges us to be patient, observant, and—most importantly—present.


The Energy of the City

We started in Kuala Lumpur, where the iconic Petronas Twin Towers dominate the skyline. Climbing the rainbow stairs at Batu Caves was a workout, but the massive golden statue of Lord Murugan at the base is a sight I’ll never forget. For a change of pace, we headed to the Cameron Highlands, where the rolling green tea plantations and cool mountain air felt like a completely different world.
